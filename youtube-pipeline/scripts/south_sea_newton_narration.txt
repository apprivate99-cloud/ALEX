Did you know Isaac Newton lost a fortune on a single stock? One of the smartest people who ever lived.

In seventeen twenty, everyone in London wanted shares in the South Sea Company. Even King George the First was its governor.

It promised huge riches from trade with South America. But Spain allowed it just one trading ship a year.

Real profits were tiny, but the hype was enormous, and insiders even bribed politicians to keep the price climbing.

Around two hundred copycat bubble companies sprang up, so the South Sea Company pushed through a law, the Bubble Act, to shut its rivals down.

As the shares took off, Newton got in early. He sold in April, and walked away with a healthy profit.

But the price kept climbing. By the summer, shares had hit around a thousand pounds, and his friends were getting rich.

Newton couldn't bear to watch from the sidelines.

So he jumped back in, just as the price was near its peak.

Not everyone was fooled. A politician named Archibald Hutcheson published pamphlets warning that the share price made no sense.

Then the bubble burst. By December, the shares had fallen right back to where they started the year.

Newton reportedly lost around twenty thousand pounds, which would be millions in today's money.

He's often quoted as saying: I can calculate the motions of the heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people.

The funny thing is, there's no solid evidence that he ever actually said it.

The lesson? Even geniuses can fall for the fear of missing out.
