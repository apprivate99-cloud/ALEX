Did you know some Germans once burned banknotes in their stoves?

In nineteen twenty three, their money was worth less than the paper it was printed on.

Just nine years earlier, in nineteen fourteen, one American dollar was worth four point two German marks.

By late nineteen twenty three, that same dollar was worth four point two trillion.

So how did this happen? After World War One, Germany owed enormous reparations.

Then, in nineteen twenty three, France and Belgium occupied the Ruhr, Germany's industrial heartland.

The workers there went on strike, and the government kept paying them by printing more and more money.

Soon, prices were rising by the hour.

Some workers were paid twice a day, and they rushed to the shops before their wages lost value.

Banknotes became so worthless that children played with bundles of them as toys, and families fed them into the fire.

By late nineteen twenty three, the mark was effectively worthless, and a lifetime of savings had been wiped out.

Finally, in November nineteen twenty three, Germany launched a new currency called the Rentenmark, swapping one new mark for one trillion old ones.

Remarkably, it worked.

The lesson? Money only works as long as people trust it.
